# History and Credits

## Tycoon Tex

Tycoon Tex is a 1985 Commodore 16 and Plus/4 game programmed by Anthony Clarke at Micro Projects for Gremlin Graphics Ltd. The surviving main source credits the concept to Matthew Clarke, Anthony Clarke, and Ian Stewart, and carries the notice `program (c) '85 micro projects`.

Anthony's recollection, recorded on 20 October 2026, is that he played Moon Patrol in pubs and elsewhere at the time. He notes that Tycoon Tex closely resembles it, but no longer recalls precisely how the concept originated. This is a personal recollection, not a claim of a documented design brief or an official arcade conversion.

The 1992 source-folder date refers to the older extraction/export. It is not the game's development date. The source notices and surviving packaging identify the game with 1985.

## Surviving Contemporary Material

The supplied artwork, cassette packaging, and press scrapbook provide contemporary context. The scrapbook's page 5 includes the Tycoon Tex cassette inlay; pages 11 and 12 contain reviews. The scans are preserved without assigning publication details that are not clearly established by the surviving cuttings.

The separate `Tycoon Tex Cassette Inlay.jpg` shows the game's cassette cover, description, and gameplay screenshots. See [Artifacts](ARTIFACTS.md) for the complete supplied collection and page references.

## Recovery Background and Build Template

The background to the original C64/1541 development setup and the later source recovery is recorded in the companion [Dorks Dilemma C16 repository](https://github.com/anthonyjclarke/Dorks_Dilemma_C16). Its [Development History](https://github.com/anthonyjclarke/Dorks_Dilemma_C16/blob/main/docs/DEVELOPMENT_HISTORY.md) contains Anthony's recollections and the recovery-tool acknowledgements.

This project adapts the Dorks Dilemma source conversion, disk reader, ca65/ld65 build, PlatformIO integration, and VICE-monitor approach. Tycoon Tex's source order, memory layout, graphics choices, original object listing, and running-reference comparison are validated separately here. The recovery narrative is intentionally maintained in the companion repository.
